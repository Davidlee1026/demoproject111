Abstract 	This study was conducted a literature review on the premonitory symptoms of sports injuries and
treatment methods in athletes. We utilized three databases: Google Scholar, PubMed, and SPORTDiscus, and
searched for additional papers through cross-references. A total of 1,174 papers were identified, and after
screening, 1,056 were excluded. A total of 118 papers were included in this literature review study. The
keywords used for the search included terms related to injury sites and tissues, types of injuries, symptoms and
signs, and treatment and management methods. The criteria for including studies in this literature review were
as follows: 1) Presentation of premonitory symptoms that could lead to sports injuries; 2) Definition of
premonitory symptoms; 3) Presentation of the relationship between premonitory symptoms and chronic sports
injuries. The premonitory symptoms for chronic sports injuries observed in this literature review were
classified into the following eight symptoms: tightness, soreness, weakness, discomfort, spasm, laxity,
swelling, and stiffness. These premonitory symptoms are risk factors for chronic injuries in the skeletal and
soft tissues of athletes. Continuous microtrauma in the musculotendinous complex manifests as symptoms
such as tightness, weakness, and spasms. Microtrauma in joints and surrounding soft tissues can cause
soreness, discomfort, laxity, swelling, and stiffness, potentially leading to chronic injuries. Eight premonitory
symptoms observed in this study are considered risk factors that lead to chronic injuries.
Key words: Chronic injury, Premonitory symptoms, Prevention, Recovery
